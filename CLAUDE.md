## Project Overview

PercuSynth is an embedded electronic percussion synthesizer project by GC Lab Chile. It contains multiple independent Arduino/ESP32-S3 firmware sketches plus a collection of webapps that **generate `.ino` files with samples embedded as `PROGMEM` arrays** (compile-time samples — no runtime loading).

### Hand-written firmwares (under `firmwares/`)

- **drum_machine_basic** — 16-step sequencer drum machine with real-time synthesis (no samples)
- **drum_ruido** — Drum machine with deliberately **noisy timbres but a clean output chain** — the guiding rule after v1 was rejected for sounding bad: the dirt is made with *synthesis*, never by breaking the audio (no bit-crush, no sample-rate decimation, no output distortion; those produce aliasing, i.e. "frequencies that belong to no note"). **Patterns are random**: no factory rhythms — each BTN1 rolls a fresh 32-step pattern (2 bars of 16ths) with the active kit's feel (`posWeight()` decides where each track may fall; the step-0 kick is forced, since a 100 % random pattern with no anchor turns to mush). **5 kits** (CYBER · DUBSTEP half-time · GLITCH · INDUSTRIAL · CAOS) live in one `KITS[5]` table where each row is a whole timbre *and* its pattern densities. The 7 tracks are kick · snare · hats · **clank** (short iron) · **metal** (anvil) · **blast** (steam/air) · bass (clean sub). Metals are built from **inharmonic partials** (1.41, 2.37, 3.14…) — that's why they read as sheet metal and not as a note — and fast pitch sweeps are deliberately avoided: a short sweep through the mid range sounds like a bird chirp, not industrial percussion (a real complaint against an earlier version). Controls are flat — **one button, one function, on the press edge; every pot always means the same thing**: BTN1 pattern · BTN2 timbre · BTN3 tap tempo · BTN4 hold = **half-time** (each pattern step lasts two master-clock steps; the master keeps running so releasing lands back in time) · BTN5 hold = fill. POT1 volume · POT2 filter · POT3 dirt · POT4 **beat repeat** OFF/×2/×4/×8/×16 (loop lengths 8/4/2/1 steps, hysteresis on the zones, anchor searches backwards for a block that actually has hits or the break becomes silence) over a free-running master clock so it never drifts. There is no play/stop — the machine always runs; POT1 to zero is the mute. The sound rules, each from a measurement: **two envelopes per hit — one for the tone, one for the noise** (a 6 ms noise transient over a 200 ms body is what separates a kick from a "pfff"); the per-voice filter touches **only the noise layer** so the kick's fundamental survives; **decays are exponential taus**, so a hit rings ~3·tau — v1's kick had tau 0.42 and rang for nearly 2 s, becoming a bass drone that masked everything (now 0.13–0.30); **PolyBLEP on every waveform with corners**; the kick lands at ~50 Hz (not 36, which no small speaker reproduces) with its own soft saturation for audible harmonics; **sidechain** ducking the rest 3 dB per kick so the kick is *felt* rather than competing; `TRACK_GAIN[]` keeps the bass at 0.62 since it shares the kick's band; filter curve `pFilt^0.55` because a pure exponential put the pot's midpoint at 1.4 kHz and made the hats (7–12 kHz) vanish, plus a **filter envelope** (each hit opens the cutoff, deeper the more closed the pot is) and resonance up to 3.2 with `Q^0.35` makeup; **two-band saturation** (split at 2.2 kHz, lows up to ×4.35, highs barely touched — saturating the highs equally is what makes the aliasing screech); global filter coefficients interpolated **per sample**; DC blocker on the output. Measured with the filter open: crest factor 2.8–3.7, peak 0.76–0.83, zero NaN, zero samples at full scale; sweeping POT3 on an isolated kick lifts sub-100 Hz by 8–9 dB while the >12 kHz band moves 1–4 dB and stays 35 dB down. 10 one-shot voices, short DMA queue (4×128 ≈ 12 ms). 6 SMD LEDs as status only. No IMU/Serial. Requires FastLED
- **drum_poder** — **Percussion only: nothing above the low end is melodic or long.** That is the firmware's defining rule, and it came from the sound being rejected twice. **No voice follows the chord — not even the sub.** The kit is *in tune with* the MIDI notes (the sub sounds the tonic of the groove's key) but nothing tracks the harmony; the kick, toms and snare body are tuned percussion with their own fixed pitch; and everything above 1 kHz (hats, metal, clap, FX) is **filtered noise** — no pitch, so it cannot play a melody. A scale-tuned FM bell up in the spectrum turns a drum machine into a melody, which is exactly what this is not. Related: a short band-passed noise burst at 2.5–4 kHz reads as a cowbell/clave/rim — latin percussion — so the only things above the hats are the plate and **noise-sweep FX** used as transitions. And a synthesized kick needs **three bands** to feel heavy: fundamental (37–52 Hz) for weight, the 2nd harmonic at real level (80–110 Hz) for the chest — the one everyone forgets, and without it the kick sounds thin on any small speaker — and the mallet for definition. **That mallet must be band-passed NOISE, never a sine**: a short sine at 1–2 kHz inside a hit is a *whistle glued to every kick*, and it survived three rejected versions of this firmware before a spectral-prominence test found it. Same rule killed the saturation on the snare and tom: saturating a voice with two partials leaves a pure tone at their difference (measured: 636 Hz at 16 dB over its neighbours on the snare, 899 Hz at 13 dB on the tom). The general test: no percussion hit may have a bin standing more than 12 dB above its third-octave neighbours above 600 Hz, and the detector must average ~8 hits with different noise seeds or a single 20 ms noise burst invents peaks of its own. Decay has a **per-band budget** (below 100 Hz tau ≤ 0.70 s; 100–250 ≤ 0.22; 250–1k ≤ 0.10; above 1 kHz ≤ 0.14, only the crash reaches 0.50), measured at −40 dB: sub 2.1 s, kick 1.3 s, tom 0.7 s, snare 0.5 s, hat 0.13 s, tick 0.07 s. The weight lives low and long; everything above is short and percussive. Also learned here: a limiter needs **lookahead** (64 samples) or the final clipper catches every transient instead; the **13 kHz master low-pass goes AFTER the limiter** (a limiter multiplies by a moving gain, and multiplying is modulating); the plate reverb must be **normalized by its own feedback** or it blooms and eats the limiter; and `Serial.setTxTimeoutMs(0)` is mandatory in any audio firmware that prints — a USB CDC write blocks until the host reads and starves the I2S DMA, which is heard as crackling on button presses. Sibling of `drum_ruido` with the sign flipped: **noise is not the aesthetic here**. The timbres go for *weight* — round kicks with a mallet knock, big tuned snares, singing toms, cymbals with controlled brightness — and they come out **mixed and mastered**, each instrument in its own frequency lane. The rhythms are **not random**: 12 **hand-written grooves** in the language of Sleep Token, Tool and Dream Theater, with real odd meters (7/8 as 3+2+2, 5/8+7/8, 9/8, 13/8, 5/4, 12/8, double-bass 4/4 and a displaced 4/4 in 3+3+3+3+2+2), crossed with electronic layers (clap, FM bell, tuned 808 sub). Patterns are written **as text**, one character per 16th and one bar per string literal (`"X-----x-----x---"`), so a groove reads at a glance and is edited without touching code; the alphabet covers ghosts/accents, double-kick bursts, flams, hat rolls, the three toms, open hats and bell/crash. **The MIDI DIN-5 carries the CLOCK, not notes** (GPIO 43, 31250 baud): MIDI Clock at 24 PPQN plus Start, and Stop+Start whenever the "1" is re-anchored (groove change, first tap of a tap-tempo series) so the external synth's sequence never ends up offset against the drums forever. **The bass lives in the synth.** That conclusion cost three rejected versions and each one taught something: (1) a hand-written bass line per groove — it doubled the kick, so it was not a bass line, it was the kick again; (2) an independent table of bass rhythms (gallop, 3-3-2, funk, offbeat) read on a selectable grid and crossed with melodic cells — each pattern was fine alone but **landed where the groove had nothing**, and against a hand-written 7/8 or 13/8 that is not a variation, it is a second machine playing on top; (3) patterns *derived from the groove's kick* — those provably fit (0 notes off the drums across 1.1 M) and were still boring, because a rule-generated bass sounds like a rule-generated bass. The general lesson: **when a generated part has to sit against hand-written material, either derive it from that material or don't generate it at all** — and often the right answer is to hand the job to the instrument that does it better and just send a clock. Two things make the clock usable: it is **counted with the audio's sample counter** (so it cannot drift from the drums — both come from one counter, not two) but **sent from the control task at 1 kHz**; sending it from the render injected the buffer's ±2.9 ms of jitter, which at 120 BPM is 14 % of a tick and reads as a wobbling arpeggio. Measured across the 12 grooves: clock tempo within **0.04 %** of the groove's BPM. The mix is fixed and is the point: per-track gain/pan/reverb-send/**lane high-pass** (snare 150 Hz, toms 70, clap 900, perc 400) so nobody invades the kick's sub, then a fixed master chain — kick sidechain → plate reverb (send 0 on the kick: wetting it is the fastest way to lose the hit) → two-band BODY saturation (POT3) → resonant low-pass FILTER (POT2) → fixed tilt EQ (lows +1.5 dB, highs −2.5 dB) → fixed bus glue compressor (1:2.2) → 30 Hz high-pass and 13 kHz low-pass → peak limiter with smoothed gain. **The controls are drum_ruido's, one for one** (BTN1 groove · BTN2 kit · BTN3 tap · BTN4 half-time hold · BTN5 fill hold; POT1 volume · POT2 filter · POT3 body · POT4 beat repeat) — no panels, no combos, no frozen pots. All of that existed only while the firmware generated the bass notes and went away with them. Rules learned by measurement in this firmware: **three envelopes per hit** (the mallet must die in 20 ms while the low body rings 300); **one drum head per instrument** — kick, snare, each tom, hi-hat, each ride zone and the sub are monophonic with a 3 ms fast-kill (the hi-hat choke is exactly this, and it's what stops a 168 BPM double-kick from stacking fourteen kicks); **only saturate what has ONE strong partial** (saturating a metal or an FM bell throws intermodulation products into other instruments' bands); the **FM modulator ratio must always be > 2** (below that the lower sideband lands at `|1-ratio|·f`, i.e. under the fundamental — with f=520 and ratio 1.41 it lands on 213 Hz, on top of the toms); **cymbals use band-pass, not high-pass** (a high-pass sends all the energy above 8 kHz, into the hats' lane); the reverb's **allpass gains must match** or it stops being an allpass and colours the tail; the reverb tail goes **dark** (~2.7 kHz in the loop) because a bright tail over the hats is exactly what reads as hiss; the **voice release threshold matters** (at −64 dB a 1.5 s tail keeps its voice busy for 11 s and the polyphony runs out in normal play; −48 dB frees it 3× sooner and the cut is inaudible); **fill hits are short** (decay ×0.40, floor of 30 ms between hits) because a roll of full-length toms is mud. And the one that produced an intermittent click at the end of a pattern: **freeing a voice at the release threshold is cutting the wave dead**, and a step is a broadband click — measured, every voice ended with a jump of −62 to −46 dBFS, and at the phrase turnaround (where the auto-fill, the crash and the pattern's own tails die almost together) several of those steps land on top of each other. The fix is two-stage: on crossing the threshold the voice is not freed, its envelope is switched to a **1.5 ms tau** and it is allowed to die to −82 dB — ~6 ms more occupancy, nothing beside a tail, and the residual step 34 dB lower (measured: hard cuts went from ~100/s to **zero**). Two more from the same hunt: **`fastKill` must freeze the attack** (the decay lives in the `else` branch of the attack ramp, so a voice killed mid-attack kept *rising*), and **an auto-fill must REPLACE the pattern, not stack on it** (several grooves write toms in the last steps, and the pattern's tom and the fill's tom landed in the same monophonic slot in the same sample; a drummer stops playing the pattern too). **But none of that was the click the user was actually hearing**, and that is the lesson: with the fill held down across all 12 grooves the simulator reported 0 steals and 0 hard cuts, so the discontinuity was not in the signal at all — it was the **time budget**. The render must deliver 128 samples every 2.9 ms into a 4-descriptor (~12 ms) DMA queue, and it shared `loop()` with the ADC reads and `FastLED.show()`; on a dense pattern (the densest groove has ~25 hits per bar) or during a fill the iteration overruns, the DMA starves and the driver — in `auto_clear` — outputs **zeros**. That is the click, and it is why it was intermittent and appeared on fast patterns and fills. The fix is architectural and is the one to copy: **audio gets its own task pinned to core 1; buttons, pots and LEDs run in a task on core 0 at 1 kHz**, and the control task never touches the sequencer or the voice pool — it leaves a request (`reqGroove`, `reqBPM`, `reqRepeatLen`…) that the audio task applies at a buffer boundary. Corollaries: **no `Serial` at all** (a USB CDC write blocks the core that makes it until the host reads — same failure mode), `readPot` takes 4 samples instead of 8 (each `analogRead` on the S3 costs tens of microseconds), and **voice stealing must not have a `dying` shortcut** — an intermediate version preferred stealing a voice that was already fading, but a just-fast-killed voice is still at nearly full amplitude (the fade is 3 ms), so overwriting it *was* the click it was meant to avoid. 24 voices, 8 tracks, no IMU. Requires FastLED

One raw `analogRead` per control pass at 1 kHz (each one costs tens of microseconds on the S3) with a one-pole filter doing the averaging, plus a per-sample smooth in the audio so a press has no steps. The **clock is clock only, never notes**: 24 PPQN at the sequencer's tempo (POT2 of panel C), Start on Play with the **"1" on step 1** (the ticks before it are discarded and Start goes out just ahead of that tick), Stop on Stop, and ticks flowing even while stopped so the external synth already has the tempo. Same rule as `drum_poder` and `oscilador_escalas_clock`: **counted in the audio task with the very counter that steps the sequencer** (integer accumulator, 6 ticks per 16th — one clock, not two) but **sent from the control task at 1 kHz**, since sending from the render injects the buffer's ±2.9 ms. The 121-LED strip is the one part that is **not** `bajo_8_pasos`': instead of only the light bullets it runs **8 effects that roll themselves** (bullets · rings · mirrored VU · sparks · rainbow · plasma · blocks · snake), a new one every 4 bars and on every Play, **never the one that was already playing**, with its palette, direction and one own parameter rolled with it so the same effect never looks the same twice — and no button for it, since all five already have a job. All eight react to the same four signals, so it does not matter which one comes up: the **step's hit** (stronger on steps 1 and 5), the **note** (which sets the colour), the **bass's real envelope** — the audio task publishes it in `g_env` once per buffer rather than the control core faking one — and the **FSR's pressure**, which deforms the effect live; tempo sets how many LEDs a step covers. The entropy comes from `micros()` at each button press, the espacio_modular rule. And the lesson that came from the strip actually cutting the audio on the bench: **not every audio dropout is the time budget.** The effects cost under 0.5 ms of the 16 ms between frames and run on the other core, so there was no underrun to find — the strip hangs off the board's 5 V, and an effect that lights all 121 LEDs at once (rainbow, plasma, blocks with the FSR pressed) asks for ~2 A and sags the rail, while the bullets never did because they light ~50 with a fading tail. Measured with the FSR at full: no frame of any of the 8 effects exceeds 300 mA and the frame-to-frame rise stays under 70 mA. (1) **The DRAG**: every kit carries, per track, the milliseconds that track enters *late* (the snare 8–30 ms). That is what makes a groove sound played rather than programmed, and it does not fit the 16th grid — at 86 BPM a 16th is 174 ms — so it lives in a separate **hit scheduler**: the block is cut at the next step boundary OR the next pending hit, whichever comes first, so a dragged hit lands on its exact sample. The user heard it as random clicks, and when he muted instrument by instrument it kept sounding — because **its panel was not an instrument**. Two rules out of it: a layer whose job is to make clicks cannot be on and audible by default, and its control must be able to take it to inaudible; and to judge one, measure audible transients, never discontinuities. (2) **The microphone must not sit in the audio critical path** (the lesson outlived the layer; it applies to `voz_fx`, `grabador_campo` and any firmware that reads I2S in). This firmware's clock is the DAC write, which blocks while the DMA queue is full. An I2S *read* paces itself too — you cannot read faster than real time — so with both in series in one loop every block took twice as long; and `leerMic()` was doing **two** reads per block to "discard the old one if the mic runs ahead", which threw away mic audio on the board and doubled the problem in the simulator. The mic now has **its own task on core 0** that reads blocking and leaves blocks in an 8-deep ring; the audio task only copies and never waits (the `grabador_campo` idea in reverse). After: **0 audio gaps** (was 200) and the worst block down from **515 %** to **97 %** of its budget, averaging **9 %**. With the General's POT4 half-way — where it lands at boot, since the pot is read from the ADC — the music is cut at ~1.7 kHz, so the record kept the **whole** top end to itself: measured, the music alone has −18.3 dB in 4–13 kHz and the record added **+3 dB** there, which is where the ear places presence, even though the layer sits 36 dB below the music in RMS. Routed **before** the master low-pass (which is also what a filter does to a real record) it now adds **+0.0 dB**. ## Build & Flash

Each firmware is a standalone Arduino sketch (`.ino`). There is no centralized build system.

1. Open the desired `.ino` file in Arduino IDE (or PlatformIO)
2. Select board: **ESP32** (or ESP32-S3 depending on hardware revision)
3. Compile and upload to the board
4. Monitor via Serial at **115200 baud** for diagnostic output

**Required Libraries:**
- ESP32 Arduino core (includes `driver/i2s_std.h`)
- `Wire.h` — I2C for MPU6050 (MIDI_Drum only)
- `USB.h` and `USBMIDI.h` — USB MIDI (MIDI_Drum only)
- `MPU6050` library (MIDI_Drum only)
- **FastLED** — the only library to install by hand; every LED firmware needs it
- **ESP32Synth** — only `secuenciador_melodico` and `secuenciador_melodico_cromatico`
- **Arduino AppleMIDI Library** (lathoub) — only `laser_midi_wifi` (ESP32-C3)

### Credentials

**No sketch may carry a WiFi password or an API key in its `.ino`.** The six AI firmwares
(`asistente_ia`, `asistente_naga`, `asistente_musical`, `sampler_ia`, `oscilador_ia`,
`compositor_ia`) and `laser_midi_wifi` each ship a
`secretos.example.h`; the user copies it to `secretos.h` in the same sketch folder. `secretos.h`
is gitignored, `secretos.example.h` is committed. The `.ino` guards the include so a missing file
fails at compile time with a readable message rather than at runtime:

```cpp
#if !__has_include("secretos.h")
  #error "Falta secretos.h: copia secretos.example.h a secretos.h y pon tus claves."
#endif
#include "secretos.h"
```

When adding a firmware that needs credentials, follow the same pattern — never a fallback default
that silently compiles. Note also that **WiFi makes ADC2 unreadable**; the four pots are already
on ADC1 (1, 2, 8, 10), so keep them there.

## Architecture

All audio firmwares follow the same pattern: a `setup()` that initializes hardware, and a `loop()` that processes inputs and fills I2S DMA buffers with 128-sample blocks (or sends MIDI events).

### drum_machine_basic
- **Voice struct** (10 polyphonic voices): dual-oscillator, LCG noise, 2-stage cascaded biquad filter state
- `triggerDrum()` — sets voice parameters per drum type (kick, snare, hihat, crash, click)
- `renderVoice()` — generates one sample: pitch sweep + noise mix + biquad filtering + envelope decay
- `biquadBP()` / `processBQ()` — Direct Form II Transposed biquad bandpass filter
- Pattern sequencer: `bool pattern[4][16]` stepped by BPM tempo from pot
- Main loop: fills 128-sample buffer → `i2s_channel_write()`

### MIDI_Drum
- No audio output — translates physical inputs to USB MIDI note events on channel 10 (GM drums)
- **Three input modalities:**
  - Buttons → MIDI notes via 5-element circular queue (`btnQueue`)
  - Piezo sensors → 15 ms peak-detection window → velocity (40–127) → note on/off
  - MPU6050 IMU → 20 ms acceleration peak window → velocity → note on/off
- `accelMag()` reads MPU6050 via I2C and returns 3-axis acceleration magnitude
- Retrigger prevention: 50 ms debounce per piezo, 25 ms per button

### synth_basico
- **Voice struct** (5 voices): single phase accumulator, exponential envelope, active/pressed flags
- `mixedWave()` — morphs smoothly between sine, square, and sawtooth based on pot reading

## Control Design

How an instrument in this project is expected to feel under the hands. These are design rules,
not preferences — follow them unless a firmware states otherwise:

- **One button, one function**, audible **on the press edge**. No waiting windows to disambiguate
  combos: fire immediately and let a combo *undo* the individual action with a ~4 ms fast-kill
- **Pots always mean the same thing** and the knob's physical position *is* the value. Frozen-pot
  panels and pick-up exist in some firmwares, but do not add them to new ones by default
- **Do not invent panels or combos** that were not asked for; prefer a fixed constant in the code
- **Nothing moves on its own** — autonomous LFOs writing to parameters the user believes they are
  holding destroy the sense of playing the instrument
- **Noisy ≠ distorted**: dirty timbres come from synthesis (inharmonic partials, filtered noise,
  band-split saturation), never from bit-crush, decimation or bus drive — those are aliasing
- Percussion checklist: **two envelopes per hit** (tone + noise) — three if there is a mallet/crack
  transient, exponential decay taus (a hit rings ~3·tau), PolyBLEP on any waveform with corners,
  DC blocker on the output, kick sidechain, **one drum head per instrument** (kick/snare/each
  tom/hi-hat/sub monophonic with a 3 ms fast-kill on retrigger), **saturate only voices with one
  strong partial** (never a metal or an FM bell: the intermodulation lands in other instruments'
  bands), **FM modulator ratios always > 2**, and watch the **voice release threshold** (−64 dB
  keeps a 1.5 s tail's voice busy for 11 s and exhausts the polyphony; −48 dB is inaudible)
- **When a generated part has to sit against hand-written material, either derive it from that
  material or don't generate it at all.** A free-standing pattern table lands where the written
  part has nothing, and against an odd meter that reads as two machines playing at once, not as
  a variation. And often the right answer is to hand the job to the instrument that does it
  better and just send it a clock (`drum_poder` lost its bass generator entirely this way, after
  three tries)
- **A click that comes and goes with pattern density is a TIME budget problem, not a signal
  problem.** Before reshaping envelopes, check the arithmetic: the render must deliver
  `BUF_SAMPLES` every `BUF_SAMPLES/44100` s into a DMA queue of `dma_desc_num` buffers, and the
  I2S driver in `auto_clear` outputs **zeros** on underrun — that is a click. `analogRead` on the
  S3 costs tens of microseconds, `FastLED.show()` and any `Serial.print` block the core that
  makes them. The fix is to give audio **its own task pinned to core 1** and run buttons/pots/LEDs
  in a task on core 0, with the control task leaving *requests* the audio task applies at a
  buffer boundary — never touching the sequencer or the voice pool itself (`drum_poder`,
  `asistente_musical`). Diagnostic: if the simulator reports 0 voice steals and 0 hard cuts and
  the board still clicks, it is this. Also: **call `FastLED.addLeds` from the LED task on core 0, not from `setup()`** —
  `setup()` runs on core 1 and the RMT interrupts follow the core that initialized them, so a long
  strip or matrix steals audio time (measured in `industrial_matriz`: peaks of 110–129 % of the
  block budget). Measure on the board with `ESP.getCycleCount()` before blaming the signal. And before cutting voices, try **`#pragma GCC optimize ("O2")`** at the top of the sketch:
  the Arduino ESP32 core builds with `-Os`, and in `industrial_matriz` O2 alone took the measured worst case
  from 65 % average / 95 % peaks to 42 % / 58 %
- **Never free a voice by setting `active = false` at the release threshold** — that cuts the wave
  dead and a step is a broadband click (measured in `drum_poder`: every voice ended with a jump of
  −62 to −46 dBFS, ~100 per second, and at a phrase turnaround several land together, which is
  exactly what an intermittent "small click at the end of the pattern" sounds like). Cross the
  threshold → switch the envelope to a ~1.5 ms tau → free the voice at −82 dB. Costs ~6 ms of
  occupancy and puts the residual step 34 dB lower. Same rule for a **fast-kill: it must freeze
  the attack**, or a voice killed mid-attack keeps rising (the decay lives in the `else` branch of
  the attack ramp), and for **an automatic fill: it REPLACES the pattern's tracks, never stacks on
  them** — the pattern's tom and the fill's tom in the same monophonic slot in the same sample is
  one hit killing the other
- Harsh treble is a design bug, not taste: **hats and cymbals get a band-pass, never a bare
  high-pass** (a high-pass passes everything up to Nyquist and that top octave is pure edge),
  keep the metallic resonance's Q moderate, put a **fixed ~13 kHz ceiling on the master**, tie
  a filter's **resonance to its cutoff** (never a resonant peak up in the treble), keep **all
  saturation before the master filter** (a nonlinearity after it re-injects highs the filter
  can no longer remove), and **smooth the limiter's gain** (~0.5 ms) — a per-sample gain step
  is a broadband click, and smoothing it also gives back 1 point of crest factor
- Do not render and send audio files of a firmware — the board is right there and gets listened to.
  To validate audio logic before flashing, compile the `.ino` on the PC against mocks of
  `Arduino.h` / `Wire.h` / `i2s_std.h` and render to a local WAV: that catches NaN, clipping,
  out-of-scale notes and stuck voices that `arduino-cli` cannot see
